## Utah's Structural Ant

Carpenter ants (*Camponotus* species) are the biggest ants Utah homeowners encounter — large workers can reach half an inch, and Utah's species range in color from solid black to red-and-black. Utah State University Extension counts around a dozen species in the state. Most nest harmlessly in stumps, logs, and landscape timbers. The problem starts when a colony moves from the yard into a structure, because the nest galleries they carve weaken the wood they occupy.

## Parent Colonies and Satellite Nests

Carpenter ants complicate control with their colony structure. A mature parent colony — usually outdoors, in a stump, dead tree limb, or buried wood — can establish satellite nests inside nearby structures. Satellites need less moisture and often occupy wall voids, hollow doors, foam insulation, and attic spaces. This is why indoor activity can persist even after an obvious outdoor nest is removed, and why tracing trails matters more than spraying them.

## Carpenter Ant or Termite Damage?

If you've found hollowed wood, the gallery tells the story:

- **Carpenter ant galleries** are smooth, clean, and sandpapered-looking, following the soft grain. The colony ejects debris, so you find frass piles nearby.
- **Subterranean [termite](/pest-library/termites) galleries** are rough, packed with soil and dried mud, and follow the soft springwood. You may also find mud shelter tubes on foundations.

Either finding is worth a professional inspection — and if a real-estate transaction is involved, a formal WDI (wood-destroying insect) inspection covers both. If what you found is a spring swarm of winged insects rather than damaged wood, the [termite page](/pest-library/termites) has the swarmer-versus-flying-ant checklist.

## Prevention Is Mostly Moisture Control

A few foragers in summer may just be scouts from an outdoor nest in a stump or fence line, but regular indoor activity, winter activity, or frass piles point to a nest in the structure — and that nest almost always started at a moisture problem. So the durable prevention is unglamorous: fix roof, gutter, and plumbing leaks promptly, keep firewood off the ground and out of long-term garage storage, trim tree limbs back from the roofline (branches are forager highways), and pull stumps and buried wood near the foundation, where parent colonies get their start.
