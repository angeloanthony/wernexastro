## The Sting Behind the Headlines

The Arizona bark scorpion (*Centruroides sculpturatus*) deserves its reputation on medical grounds: it is the one scorpion in the United States whose sting is a genuine medical event, especially for young children. In its core range in Arizona it is a fixture of home life. Utah sits at the very northern edge of that range, and the documented picture here is much narrower.

## What the Evidence Actually Shows

Utah's bark scorpion records follow the Colorado River drainage in the far south of the state, with Kane County as the mainly cited stronghold. That's the honest boundary of current authoritative documentation. It is not proof the species can never turn up elsewhere — ranges shift, and commerce moves animals — but it does mean the common claim that bark scorpions are an established fact of St. George life isn't supported by the university research we can cite. When a St. George homeowner brings us "a bark scorpion," it is nearly always one of the species below.

## The Scorpions You're Actually Finding

- **[Giant desert hairy scorpion](/pest-library/desert-hairy-scorpion)** — Utah's largest scorpion and Washington County's most conspicuous one. Big, robust, painful sting, mild venom.
- **[Northern scorpion](/pest-library/northern-scorpion)** — smaller, ground-dwelling, and effectively the only scorpion across most of northern Utah, including the Uintah Basin.
- **Other natives** — several smaller ground and burrowing scorpions occur in Utah's deserts; robust pincers and ground habits separate all of them from *Centruroides*.

## Why Identification Is Worth Five Minutes

The species changes the medical calculus. A household with small children in Utah's far south should treat any slender, wall-climbing scorpion with real caution and get stings evaluated quickly; the same household, looking at a heavy-bodied [desert hairy scorpion](/pest-library/desert-hairy-scorpion), is dealing with a bee-sting-class animal. Five minutes with a photo and the build test above is the difference between those two situations — which is worth more than any assumption made from the name alone.
