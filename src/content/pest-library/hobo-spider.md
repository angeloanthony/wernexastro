## The Rise and Fall of a Villain

For a generation, the hobo spider (*Eratigena agrestis*) was the Intermountain West's boogeyman — an "aggressive house spider" whose bite supposedly rotted flesh. Nearly every part of that story has fallen apart under study. The aggression was misread escape behavior; the bite evidence didn't replicate; and the CDC-era case reports that built the reputation mostly lacked a confirmed spider. Today USU Extension writes plainly that significant evidence for necrotic hobo bites doesn't exist. The spider stayed the same; the facts improved.

## The Spider, Minus the Myth

The hobo is a funnel weaver — it builds a flat silk sheet with a retreat tunnel at ground level and waits for insects to stumble across it. It's a poor climber, which is why encounters happen on floors, in window wells, and in the smooth-sided traps of tubs and basement sinks rather than on walls and ceilings. Northern Utah, the Basin included, suits it perfectly; hot Dixie does not.

## The Identification Trap

Brown + fast + basement = "hobo!" is northern Utah reflex, but the same description fits giant house spiders, other funnel weavers, and wolf spiders — all harmless. Since a certain ID needs a microscope and the medical stakes are low, the practical question isn't "is this a hobo?" but "is this a [black widow](/pest-library/black-widow)?" — shiny, black, hourglass, hanging in a messy low web. That's the one Utah spider worth genuine caution. And if someone tells you it was a [brown recluse](/pest-library/brown-recluse), the answer in Utah is no.

## The Only Date That Matters Is August

Almost everyone deals with hobo spiders in October, which is the one month the effort is wasted. By then the males are already inside, the population outdoors is unchanged, and killing the spider in the bathtub accomplishes exactly one spider's worth of good.

The work that matters happens in late August, before the males start moving. Tight door sweeps on exterior and garage doors are the single highest-value item — a wandering ground spider that cannot get under a door is a spider you never meet. Sealing foundation cracks and window-well frames closes the rest. Clearing dense ground cover, rock piles, and clutter off the immediate foundation line removes the funnel-web habitat that concentrates them against the house in the first place.

Do that in August and September is quiet. Skip it and September is a parade, which is roughly the whole story of this spider in a northern Utah basement.
