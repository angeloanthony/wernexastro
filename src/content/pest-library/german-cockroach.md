## The Roach That Means Business

Utah's cockroach lineup includes several species, but the German cockroach is the one that turns a sighting into an infestation. It cannot survive Utah winters outdoors and doesn't try — it is a permanent indoor resident, moving between buildings inside boxes and appliances. That indoor-only life is also its weakness: everything it needs (food residue, water, warm harborage) is under the homeowner's and technician's control.

## Where They Hide

German cockroaches are thigmotactic — they want tight cracks touching both their back and belly. By day the population is invisible, wedged into cabinet hinge gaps, under sink rims, behind drawer slides, in appliance motor housings, and inside wall penetrations around plumbing. A useful home test: walk into the dark kitchen at night and switch on the light. Roaches scattering from the counter or sink area indicate an active population many times larger than what you saw.

## Why Retail Sprays Keep Failing

The German cockroach's egg-case habit defeats contact sprays — the female carries the case until just before hatching, so protected eggs simply hatch after the spray dries and the population rebuilds. Worse, repellent products drive roaches out of treated areas into untreated ones, spreading a kitchen problem through the walls of a house. Effective control instead targets the harborages where roaches actually spend their day, and keeps at it long enough to catch the generations still hatching. That patience, plus the sanitation and moisture work below, is what ends an infestation.

## Water First, Then Everything Else

Homeowners reach for food storage first, and food matters — but water is the tighter constraint. A German cockroach can live for weeks on the grease film behind a stove; it cannot go nearly as long without a drink. Fix the drip under the sink, wipe the basin dry before bed, empty the pet bowl after dinner, and you have applied more pressure than most of what comes in a can.

Food and clutter come next, and they are the same problem twice. Crumbs in and under appliances feed the population; cardboard in the pantry both harbors it and, very often, delivered it. Inspecting secondhand appliances and furniture before they cross the threshold is the cheapest control measure in this entire library, because for a species that cannot walk in from the yard, arrival is the only moment the problem is free to prevent.
