## The Ant You're Probably Looking At

Utah has plenty of ant species, but if small brown ants are trailing through your kitchen or garage — especially in the northern half of the state — the odds strongly favor the pavement ant (*Tetramorium immigrans*). It earned its name honestly: colonies nest in soil under slabs, sidewalks, driveways, and stones, pushing the excavated sand up through cracks in little volcano-shaped piles. Development suits them perfectly — every new subdivision is a grid of ideal nest sites.

## Why Slab Homes Get Hit Hardest

A pavement ant colony under a heated slab has everything: stable temperature, protection, and moisture. From there, foragers reach interior floors through the gaps every slab has — expansion joints, plumbing penetrations, settling cracks. Trails typically appear along baseboards and counter backsplashes nearest the entry point. They'll take nearly anything edible, with a preference for greasy and sweet foods, and they recruit fast: one scout finding crumbs becomes a trail by morning.

## Doing Your Part

- Follow the trail before wiping it up — knowing the entry crack is genuinely useful information for treatment.
- Keep counters and floors clear of crumbs and standing water; rinse recycling.
- Seal visible gaps around plumbing penetrations and along baseboards near known entry points.
- Resist the repellent-spray habit: sprays scatter trails and make colonies harder to bait, without touching the queen.

## Lookalikes Worth Ruling Out

If the ants are noticeably large or emerging with wings from wood, think [carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ant). If you found a big bare gravel mound in the yard rather than sand piles in pavement cracks, see [harvester ants](/pest-library/harvester-ant). Size is the quick separator: pavement ants are small and uniform; both of those are far larger.
