## Utah's Native Mound Builder

Harvester ants (*Pogonomyrmex* species) are big, reddish, conspicuous ants that live in one of the most recognizable nests in the West: a low gravel disk, sometimes several feet across, at the center of a circle of ground the ants have stripped bare of vegetation. That clearing isn't decoration — it keeps sight lines open and the nest sun-warmed. Colonies live for years, sending foragers out along trunk trails to collect seeds, which they husk and store in underground granaries.

## Why They Get Called Fire Ants

Big red ants plus a painful sting equals "fire ants" in most people's minds — but true red imported fire ants are a southeastern US species that is not established in Utah. The mix-up matters for two reasons. First, panic-driven treatments aimed at "fire ants" tend to be the wrong ones. Second, harvester ants are native and ecologically useful; a colony that isn't threatening anyone doesn't automatically need to die. When the mound is beside a swing set, that calculation changes, and that's a legitimate treatment call.

## Living With (or Without) Them

- Give mounds a wide berth during yard work; stings cluster around nest disturbance.
- Teach kids to recognize the bare-circle mound — it's the ant equivalent of a "keep out" sign.
- On larger properties, mowing around rather than over active mounds avoids provoking defenders.
- If a mound is in a high-traffic or play area, treat the colony properly once rather than skirmishing with it all summer.

## Not Your Mound?

Small sand piles in sidewalk cracks are [pavement ants](/pest-library/pavement-ant). Big ants coming from wood, or indoors in winter, are [carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ant). The bare gravel disk in open sun is the harvester signature.
